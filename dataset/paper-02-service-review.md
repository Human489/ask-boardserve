# St Ambrose Hospice Care

## Board Paper: Ashcombe Day Therapy Unit, Closure and Repurposing Plan

**Meeting: Board, 17 March 2026. Agenda item 7.**
**Author: Chief Executive. Purpose: to approve.**
**Raised as an action by the Board on 17 September 2025.**

### Recommendation

That the Board approves the closure of the Ashcombe day therapy unit with effect from 1 September 2026, approves the repurposing of the building as a community palliative care hub, and approves the communications and consultation approach at appendix C.

### Background

The Ashcombe unit opened in 1998 and provides a day therapy service on three days a week from a leased building two miles from the main hospice site. It is staffed by 2.4 whole time equivalent nurses, one occupational therapist at 0.6, and eighteen volunteers.

The Board asked in September 2025 for a costed options paper including a closure scenario. That paper was received in November 2025. This paper brings the recommendation arising from it.

### The case for change

Attendance has fallen from an average of 22 people per session in 2019 to 9 per session in the twelve months to December 2025. Of those nine, an average of 3.1 travel more than twelve miles. The unit costs £412,000 a year to run, of which £68,000 is the lease, which expires in August 2027 with a break at August 2026.

Cost per attendance is now £86 against a benchmark of £41 for comparable day services in the region. The clinical view, set out at appendix A, is that the model of care has changed: the majority of people who would once have attended day therapy are now supported at home by the community team, and those who attend Ashcombe would be better served by a service designed around outreach rather than a building.

Three options were assessed:

1. **Continue unchanged.** Cost £412,000 a year. Attendance likely to continue falling. Rejected.
2. **Consolidate onto the main site.** Saves the lease, £68,000, but the clinical view is that a building based model is the problem, not the building. Partially adopted: two of the three sessions will run from the main hospice as a transitional arrangement to March 2027.
3. **Close and repurpose as a community hub.** Recommended. Net saving £180,000 in 2026/27 and £294,000 in a full year, with the balance reinvested in two additional community nurse specialist posts.

### Impact on people using the service

Thirty-one people attended at least once in the last quarter. Each will receive an individual assessment and a named contact before any change takes effect, and the Board is asked to note that no person will lose support without an alternative in place. Appendix B sets out the transition plan person by person, in anonymised form.

Feedback from the six week engagement with current attenders and their families was mixed. Twelve of nineteen respondents said the social contact mattered more to them than the therapy. The plan responds with a volunteer-led weekly social group at the main site, funded within the saving.

### Impact on staff and volunteers

No redundancies are proposed. The 2.4 nursing whole time equivalents transfer into the community team, where there are current vacancies. The occupational therapy post is at risk and consultation will follow the hospice's policy. Volunteers will be offered roles at the main site; the paper notes that volunteer numbers across the hospice have fallen 19 per cent since 2023 and that some attrition should be expected.

### Risks

The principal risks are reputational, given the local profile of the unit and the involvement of a well known donor family in its original funding, and clinical, if the community team cannot absorb the transferred caseload while carrying vacancies. Mitigations are at appendix D. The Board should note that the reputational risk is not fully mitigable and that some local criticism should be expected.

### Financial effect

Full year saving £294,000. In-year saving 2026/27 £180,000, assuming a 1 September implementation. One-off costs of £46,000 for dilapidations and moving. The saving is assumed in the 2026/27 budget approved in March.

### Consultation and approvals

The clinical governance committee reviewed the clinical case on 11 February 2026 and supported it. The executive team approved the recommendation on 3 March 2026. Legal advice on the lease break has been taken and the break notice must be served by 28 February 2026, which has been done on a protective basis and can be withdrawn by agreement if the Board does not approve.

### Decision sought

Approval of the closure, the repurposing, and the communications approach.
