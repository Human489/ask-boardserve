# St Ambrose Hospice Care

## Board Paper: Trustee Skills Audit and Board Composition

**Meeting: Board, 17 March 2026. Agenda item 9.**
**Author: Chair, supported by the Company Secretary. Purpose: to discuss and agree next steps.**
**Raised as an action by the Board on 17 November 2025.**

### Recommendation

That the Board notes the results of the skills audit, agrees the two recruitment priorities set out in section 4, and asks the Chair to bring a succession plan to the May meeting.

### 1. Method

All ten trustees completed a self assessment against ten skill areas in January 2026, scoring themselves 1 to 5 where 1 is no knowledge and 5 is expert or professional level. Self assessment has known limitations and the Board should read the results as a conversation starter rather than as measurement. The full matrix is at appendix A and is held by the Company Secretary.

### 2. What the audit shows

The Board scores strongly on strategy, stakeholder and communications, and sector or operational expertise. Six trustees score 4 or 5 on strategy and the average across the Board is above 3.

Three areas are weak.

**Digital and data** is the weakest area on the Board. Only one trustee scores 5, and half the Board scores 2 or below. This matters now rather than in the abstract, because the electronic patient record replacement is the largest non-property investment the hospice will make in this plan period, and because the November 2025 phishing incident showed that the Board could not independently judge the assurance it was given.

**Estates and assets** is thin. Two trustees score 4 or 5. The hospice faces a £1.4m roof replacement, a lease break decision at Ashcombe and a seventeen shop retail estate under review.

**Governance practice** is mixed rather than weak, with a wide spread. Four trustees score 4 or 5, four score 2 or below. The spread matters more than the average, because it means the burden of governance judgement falls on a small group.

### 3. Composition and tenure

The Board has ten trustees against a constitutional maximum of twelve, and two vacancies. Average tenure is 4.5 years. Two trustees have served more than eight years and both reach the nine year limit within eighteen months, including the trustee who chairs no committee but sits on two.

The Chair has served six and a half years, with a maximum term of nine. The Chair of Finance and Audit has served four years. There is currently no written succession plan for any of these roles. The Board asked for one in January 2026; it is overdue and the Chair takes responsibility for that.

### 4. Recruitment priorities

The Chair proposes two priorities for the current vacancies:

1. **A trustee with digital, data or technology leadership experience**, ideally with experience of a clinical systems implementation. This addresses the weakest area on the matrix and the most significant investment in front of the hospice.
2. **A trustee with recent clinical or palliative care practice experience.** The Clinical Governance Committee currently relies on one trustee with current clinical knowledge, and the Board should not have a single point of failure on its most important area of assurance.

Estates expertise, though thin, is proposed to be met by co-option to the Finance and Audit Committee rather than by a trustee appointment, because the need is time limited.

### 5. Diversity

The Board is ten people. Three are under 45. Three are from a minority ethnic background. The gender balance is six to four. No trustee has lived experience of using hospice services as a carer or family member, so far as the Board is aware, and none has been recruited on that basis. The Chair invites the Board to consider whether that is a gap.

### 6. Development

No trustee has an individual development plan. Trustee appraisals were last carried out in 2024 and consisted of a conversation with the Chair without a written record. The external board effectiveness review was carried out in 2024 and its fifteen recommendations have not been reported on since; that action is overdue.

The Chair proposes that individual appraisals resume in the autumn, that each trustee leaves with one development objective, and that the Board commissions its next external review for 2027.

### 7. What the Board is asked to do

Agree the two recruitment priorities, agree the co-option approach for estates, and note that a succession plan will come to the May meeting.
